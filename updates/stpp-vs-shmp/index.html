STPP vs SHMP: What Is the Difference?

STPP (sodium tripolyphosphate, Na5P3O10) and SHMP (sodium hexametaphosphate, (NaPO3)6) are two of the most common phosphates in detergent and water treatment formulations. Buyers often ask whether one can replace the other. They are related chemicals, but they do different jobs — this article explains the difference in practical terms.

What Each Product Does

STPP is primarily a builder. It softens water by binding hardness ions, suspends soil, and provides alkaline buffering — a combination of functions in a single ingredient, which is why it became the standard detergent builder.

SHMP is primarily a sequestrant and dispersant. It is especially effective at keeping calcium and magnesium dispersed at low dosage, preventing scale formation, and deflocculating suspended solids. It works at threshold levels — small amounts control much larger amounts of hardness.

Where Each Is Used

Detergents and cleaning: STPP is the main builder in powder laundry detergents and many cleaning formulations. SHMP appears in some liquid and specialty cleaners, mainly for water softening and scale control rather than as the principal builder.

Water treatment: SHMP is the common choice for scale and corrosion control in cooling and boiler-related systems. STPP is used in some water treatment and dispersing roles but is less typical as the primary anti-scale agent.

Industrial processing: SHMP is widely used as a deflocculant in ceramics and as a dispersant in coatings and drilling. STPP is used in ceramics, paper and textile processing as well. For a fuller picture, see what STPP is used for and what SHMP is used for.

Food applications: Both products exist in food grades — food grade STPP for moisture retention in meat and seafood, and food grade SHMP as a quality improver and sequestrant — each with its own permitted roles and standards.

Key Specification Differences

The headline specification also differs: STPP is usually quoted on Na5P3O10 assay and P2O5 around 57%, while SHMP is quoted on total phosphate as P2O5, typically 68% for technical grade. Form is similar (white powder or granular), but dissolution behavior and pH of solution differ, which matters in formulation.

How to Choose

If you are formulating a powder detergent, STPP is normally the starting point. If your problem is scale, dispersion or water softening at low dosage, SHMP is usually the better tool.
